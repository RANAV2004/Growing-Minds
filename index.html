About the therapist

Therapy that feels like play and works in real life.

Occupational therapy helps children do the everyday things that matter to them: playing, learning, dressing, eating, writing, making friends and managing big feelings. Signs your child may benefit from OT

Every child grows at their own pace, but these are worth a conversation:

  • Missing milestones like sitting, crawling, walking or holding objects
  • Struggles with handwriting, colouring, scissors or buttons
  • Very sensitive to noise, clothes, food textures or being touched
  • Frequent meltdowns, difficulty calming down or switching tasks
  • Seems clumsy, falls often or avoids playground play
  • Finds it hard to sit, focus or follow instructions in class
  • Very picky eating or difficulty chewing and swallowing

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What is occupational therapy for children?

Paediatric occupational therapy helps children develop the skills they need for daily life play, self-care, learning and social participation. It works on things like motor skills, sensory processing, attention and independence, usually through structured play.

Which therapies are available at the clinic?

Growing Minds offers Occupational Therapy, Speech & Language Therapy, Communication Therapy, Special Education and Applied Behaviour Analysis (ABA). Many children benefit from a combination, and after the assessment we'll suggest the right mix for your child.

What age group do you work with?

We see children from 6 months to 15 years of age from babies with delayed milestones to teenagers needing support with learning, behaviour or daily skills.

Do I need a doctor's referral or diagnosis?

No. You can book directly. If you already have reports from a paediatrician, neurologist or psychologist, please bring them to the first visit.

What happens in the first session?

The first visit is an assessment. Dr. Khyati will talk with you about your concerns, observe and play with your child, and check motor, sensory and daily-living skills. You'll then discuss goals and a recommended therapy plan.

How long is each session and how often?

Therapy sessions are usually around 45 minutes. Most children attend 2–3 times a week, but the frequency depends on your child's needs and goals and is agreed with you after the assessment.

What should my child wear or bring?

Comfortable clothes that allow free movement, a water bottle, and any glasses or orthotics your child uses. For handwriting concerns, bring a school notebook.

Can parents stay during the session?

Yes. Parent involvement is encouraged, and we'll show you activities to continue at home. For some children, part of the session may work better one-to-one, which we'll discuss with you.

Do you offer online video consultations?

Yes. You can book an online video call on WhatsApp Video, Google Meet or Zoom during clinic hours. Online sessions work well for first consultations, parent coaching, home-program reviews and follow-ups. Hands-on therapy and detailed assessments are best done in the clinic.

How do I reschedule or cancel?
